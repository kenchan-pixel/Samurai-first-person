# Product Goal

## Vision

Create a mobile-first, first-person 3D action game in which the player embodies a Japanese samurai and survives a sequence of cinematic duels using timing, directional reading, parries, and katana counterattacks.

The experience should feel immediate and physical on a phone:

- enemy intent is readable through animation rather than dense UI;
- tapping different regions of the screen produces directional blocks;
- swiping produces directional katana attacks;
- success depends more on timing and observation than rapid button mashing;
- every stage introduces a meaningfully different enemy, rhythm, arena pressure, or combat rule;
- visual, audio, haptic, and motion feedback should make each parry and strike satisfying;
- the game should progressively become richer than the initial brief while remaining coherent, learnable, performant, and playable with one hand or two thumbs.

## Experience pillars

1. **Read the opponent** — posture, sword path, gaze, movement, sound, and environment communicate danger.
2. **Commit at the right moment** — early, late, and wrong-direction reactions have different consequences.
3. **Directional physicality** — screen location and swipe direction map naturally to defensive and offensive motion.
4. **Distinct duels** — enemies are not merely larger health bars; each has recognisable behaviours and openings.
5. **Mobile clarity** — the main view remains unobstructed and every essential action works without tiny controls.
6. **Visible evolution** — recurring autonomous improvement runs must create substantial, observable progress.

## Long-term outcome

A polished browser game with a compact campaign, memorable bosses, expressive combat animation, replayable scoring/challenge modes, strong mobile performance, and enough systemic depth to support continued expansion without redesigning the core interaction model.

## Non-goals for the initial experiment

- Open world exploration.
- Multiplayer or live service systems.
- Loot economy, gacha, monetisation, or account systems.
- Photorealistic historical simulation.
- Copying a named commercial game's characters, levels, assets, or combat implementation.
